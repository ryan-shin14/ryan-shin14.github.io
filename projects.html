The Cartesian Hand
Electrical Stack, Control, Hardware · 2026
A 7-DoF end-effector that combines independent grasping and relative manipulation using only linear motion. Two independently actuated parallel grippers hold different parts of an object while four translating fingertips move those parts relative to each other, so tasks like cap opening and closing, pipetting, pumping, screwdriving, trigger actuation, and in-grasp reorientation can be composed from simple linear motion primitives. It was demonstrated on 35 laboratory, manufacturing, and household objects.

I designed the hand’s electronics, developed its firmware and contact-aware joint control, and deployed it on a Franka FR3 arm and on Duke Humanoid V2, where two Cartesian Hands perform bimanual, sequenced lab tasks such as uncapping a tube, pipetting, and recapping.

Duke Humanoid V2
Electrical Stack, Control, Hardware · 2026
A custom 31-degree-of-freedom humanoid platform with independently actuated RGB-D cameras, built to characterize the visible-reachable workspace (VRW): where a robot can both reach and see its targets. In the accompanying study, camera articulation raised visible-reachable coverage from 38% to 97% and reduced manipulation task time by 17% and energy use by 19%.

Robodog Quadruped
Design, Hardware, Simulation & Control
A fully functional 8-DOF quadruped robot inspired by Boston Dynamics robots, designed and built from the ground up for locomotion research, simulation, and embedded robotics development.

The robot has a four-legged architecture powered by eight ST3215 serial bus servos, with two actuated joints (hip and knee) per leg. A custom chassis and leg assemblies were designed in Fusion and 3D printed in PLA for a lightweight yet durable platform. An Orange Pi running Linux serves as the onboard computer, communicating with the servos and executing locomotion algorithms developed in Python.

The completed quadruped demonstrated stable forward and backward walking as well as turning. Through iterative mechanical design, gait tuning, and testing in MuJoCo, it achieved reliable locomotion on flat surfaces. The project validated the full robotics pipeline, from CAD design and simulation to hardware integration and real-world deployment via a tuned PPO policy.

Autonomously Regulating HABs by Mobilizing UV-Pump Filtration
Design, Hardware, Sensing & Machine Learning
Harmful algal blooms (HABs) pose a growing threat to aquatic ecosystems and public health, yet current monitoring solutions are either too expensive, ecologically disruptive, or ineffective for inland and early-stage blooms. I designed and built an autonomous floating probe that uses low-cost sensors to detect dangerous algae concentrations in real time and automatically activates a UV-C filtration system to bring levels back down to safe, non-zero concentrations, preserving ecological balance while removing the need for manual sampling.

The system is built around an Elegoo UNO R3 microcontroller paired with three sensors: a SEN0189 turbidity sensor (light scattering in NTU), a CQRobot TDS sensor (conductivity), and a TCS3200 color sensor (RGB density). A 5W UV-C bulb is wired through a WWZMDiB servo acting as a programmatic switch, so the microcontroller can activate the bulb when algae concentrations cross a defined threshold. The full system (sensors, microcontroller, UV-C unit, and a water pump) is mounted on a pool noodle buoy frame for real-world deployment testing.

All four sensor metrics (turbidity, TDS, temperature, and RGB color) returned ANOVA p-values well below 0.05, confirming each could statistically distinguish between algae concentration levels. The integrated UV-C system achieved 95% activation accuracy at the 4.25 NTU threshold, triggering correctly at high concentrations and staying off at safe ones. In full prototype testing, the floating buoy reduced algae concentration by approximately 50% over 30 hours. A Random Forest classifier trained on the collected sensor data reached about 95% accuracy in classifying bloom vs. non-bloom conditions.
